One voice ships, and it isn’t privileged: drop a Piper .onnx and its .onnx.json into the voices folder and it appears in the picker. Install one named the same as the bundled voice and yours wins.

  1. Record 30–60 minutes of your own clean speech, reading prepared text. Consistency beats quantity — one microphone, one room, one session.
  2. Cut it into clips at silence, never mid-word, and don’t trim tight. This is the step that decides whether it sounds like you, and the one most guides cover in a sentence.
  3. Fine-tune from a pretrained checkpoint. Measure iterations per second over one partial epoch before committing days — a wrong batch size doesn’t fail, it quietly takes a month.
  4. Export to ONNX, install, and measure it. The pause figures are per voice and don’t transfer.

The full guide ships inside the app and carries this project’s own measurements rather than generic advice — including the batch-size cliff that cost seventeen times the training speed for one power of two.
